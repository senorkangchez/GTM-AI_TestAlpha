Graph design · messy cases

The graph earns trust when it makes the ambiguous cases explicit.

Many-to-many relationships

One opportunity can involve several products, features, and campaigns.

Keep each relationship as an edge and label the attribution rule instead of forcing one “primary” answer.

Campaign attribution

Conversion is a measured outcome, not proof of causation.

Show enrollment, conversion, and the trigger source separately; compare signal-triggered and manual cohorts without overstating lift.

Sparse data

Not every graph slice supports a rate.

Suppress views below five closed opportunities and surface density warnings when a team, segment, or territory is too granular.

Uncertain signals and ownership

Ambiguous or unverified nodes should not drive a recommendation.

Preserve evidence provenance, drop unquotable extraction claims, and route cross-functional recommendations to the owning team.

This is what turns a useful visualization into an operating model: clear entities, declared relationships, measured outcomes, and visible guardrails.

Credibility close

Macro recommendations stay credible because they can drill back to the deal — and the exact quote.

Account proof
The account view keeps the two dials — Health and Progression — and shows source, signal, timestamp, and verbatim evidence. It proves the data is trustworthy without making one deal the headline.
Guardrails
Extraction is the only LLM step. Unquotable claims are dropped; downstream scoring, aggregation, and routing are deterministic and tested. No CRM write or campaign is fired from this demo.
